What exactly do you get?

The wrong screen's problem does not show on installation day: slats spaced too far apart allow a clear view at an angle, ordinarily spray-painted iron rusts from the first winter in Jeddah's salty air, and light fabric tears in seasonal winds. So every screen with us starts from two questions: from which direction do eyes come, and from which direction does the wind come? Their answers set the screen type, height, slat angle and fixing method — before we talk about colour and pattern.

We build iron slat (louvre) and woven screens, laser-cut screens combining privacy with beauty, Lexan screens for those who want light without sight, and fabric screens for fast economical solutions — for courtyards, rooftops, pools and entrances, at considered heights that respect your privacy, your neighbour's, and building rules.

Which screen should you choose? For maximum privacy with ventilation: iron slats at a calculated angle are our first choice for most villas. For the facade and entrance where looks matter: laser-cut is worth its difference. Around pools and seating: Lexan gives you light and privacy together. For tight budgets or fast privacy: fabric — including French fabric starting from SAR 130 as published on our Google profile — but honestly: fabric life is shorter than iron, so do not buy it if you want a ten-year solution.

A detail both legal and practical: screen height. A screen that is too tall may breach setback and height rules and invite a neighbour complaint; too short and it fails against the neighbour's upper windows. At inspection we stand in your courtyard and look with the neighbour's eye from every direction and window, setting the height that genuinely blocks sight within what is permitted — real privacy with no later problems.

And for pool screens: note that a screen also blocks wind, raising humidity around the pool and affecting water evaporation and treatment chemicals. Louvre slats at the right angle block the eye while passing a measured breeze — which is why we prefer them around pools over solid sheets.

How is the price calculated?

A screen's price depends first on its type (fabric, Lexan, iron slats or woven, laser-cut), then on length, height, pattern density and the existing fence's condition. Questions before you book

Which screen gives complete privacy?

Iron slats at an angle calculated for your site, woven iron, or laser-cut with suitable pattern density. At inspection we test sight lines from neighbours' windows and choose the type and height that genuinely block the view.

Is the French fabric screen good?

Good as a fast economical solution starting from SAR 130 as published on our Google profile, but its life is inevitably shorter than iron. If you want a ten-year screen, powder-coated iron is the right investment.

Will an iron screen rust in Jeddah?

Not with us — under warranty: galvanised steel with oven-baked powder coating, the resistance pairing designed for coastal climates, and the 10-year warranty certificate covers manufacturing and installation defects and colour change.

What is the right screen height?

Set by actual sight lines from neighbours and within permitted rules — we measure it at the free inspection and do not apply one height to every house.

How long does a villa screen take?

After type and measurements are approved, fabrication in the workshop (cutting and oven paint) then installation within a few days depending on length — dense laser-cut needs slightly longer fabrication, and the schedule is fixed in the quote.
