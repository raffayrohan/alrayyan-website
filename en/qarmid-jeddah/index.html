What exactly do you get?

Most qarmid roof failures we repair are not from the tiles but from what is beneath them: missing or torn waterproofing, too little slope so water pools, and random fixing that seasonal winds break tile by tile. So we build qarmid as a system: a galvanised structure at a calculated slope, a continuous water and thermal insulation layer with no exposed joints, then tiles fixed with rust-resistant screws and clips — with edges, valleys and chimneys detailed before they are seen.

We install qarmid on annex roofs, rooftop rooms, villas, facades and shades — in Italian clay for those wanting authenticity and the longest life, and in metal for those wanting light weight, colour variety and faster execution. Italian clay or metal? Clay is heavier, costlier and longer-lived, its natural colour never changes, and it is the choice for street-visible roofs and luxury villas — but it needs a stronger structure and longer installation. Metal is lighter, faster and wider in colours, suiting annexes, light-structure roofs and tighter budgets, with a good life — though not clay's life. At inspection we check your existing roof structure first: if it cannot safely carry clay's weight, we will not install it however much you want it — we will propose metal, or structure strengthening as a clear line in the quote.

One winter's experience reveals a qarmid roof's quality: wind-driven rain in Jeddah enters horizontally under tiles if edges and valleys are not sealed. So we care for side closure pieces and insulation strips at every wall junction, and we water-test the roof before handover where access allows — a simple check that saves you discovering a leak over your furniture.

And if beneath your roof is a sandwich panel room — ours or another's — we coordinate the qarmid with the room's insulation as one system: qarmid protecting insulation, insulation protecting the room — and one responsibility with us instead of two contractors blaming each other at the first leak.

How is the price calculated?

Qarmid pricing is calculated from its type (Italian clay or metal), roof area and slope, the existing structure's condition and the need for full new insulation. We give no per-metre price by phone because two roofs of equal area can differ twofold in price due to structure and insulation. Inspection is free: we check structure, slope and existing insulation, then an itemised written quote — tiles, structure, insulation and installation — fixed once you sign.

Pricing factors

  • Tile type: authentic Italian clay or metal
  • Roof area, slope and number of valleys and edges
  • Existing structure: sufficient, needs strengthening, or replacement
  • Insulation: full new layers or local repair
  • Roof access difficulty and building height

Questions before you book

Does qarmid leak over time?

The tiles themselves do not leak; leaks come from missing insulation, wrong slope or unsealed edges. We build insulation, slope and details as one system with an official 10-year warranty covering workmanship defects.

Which is better for my roof: Italian clay or metal?

It depends on your roof structure, budget and the roof's street visibility. Clay lives longer and looks richer; metal is lighter and faster. At inspection we check the structure and tell you what suits your roof specifically.

Do you install qarmid over a sandwich panel room?

Yes — coordinated with the room's insulation as one system, the roof protecting the insulation; among our most requested annex jobs in Jeddah.

How long does a qarmid roof take?

By area, type and structure condition, with the schedule fixed in the quote. Clay is slower than metal due to its weight and fixing precision.

Does the work include removing old qarmid?

Yes on rebuilds: removing the old, inspecting beneath it, and treating structure and insulation — stated as a clear line in the quote before starting.
